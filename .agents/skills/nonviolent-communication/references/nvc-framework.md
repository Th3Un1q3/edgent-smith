# Reference: NVC Framework — 4 Components and Giraffe vs Jackal

Grounded in l7TONauJGfc, Rosenberg SF Workshop 2015 (3h, 49,980 chars, `cache/youtube-videos/nvc/l7TONauJGfc.txt`).

When to load: you need the philosophy, component definitions, or video-anchored phrasing for any rewrite.

## Vocabulary

- **Jackal** — power-over language that evaluates, blames, or coerces; plays "who's right" and punishes.
- **Giraffe** — power-with language from the animal with the largest heart of any land mammal; connects to make natural giving possible.
- **observation** — what a camera records: exact words, time, place, action, without evaluation.
- **feeling** — body-based emotion (angry, scared, disappointed, hurt, anxious, frustrated, lonely); excludes faux-feelings that smuggle judgment.
- **faux-feeling** — phrase "I feel ___" where blank implies what someone did to you (misunderstood, manipulated, judged, rejected, abandoned).
- **need** — universal human requirement (serenity, order, safety, respect, understanding, connection); contains no person and no strategy.
- **strategy** — concrete action to meet a need (clean sink, don't break windows); not a need itself.
- **request** — positive, concrete, present, doable action asked with choice ("would you be willing to…?"); contrast **demand** which punishes "no".

## Philosophy

Rosenberg's purpose: remember how to connect so natural giving becomes possible. Two games compete: "make life wonderful" (Giraffe, natural giving) versus "who's right" (Jackal, punishment and reward). Schools and families often teach Jackal — Detroit dialect, professional Jackal degree, Giraffe schools as alternative. Choose Giraffe to leave power-over and act with a big heart.

## The 4 Components

### 1. Observation Without Evaluation

Separate what happened from the story about it. Use the camera test and quote exact words.

- Jackal: "You have a big mouth" (teacher example), "You yell at me" (brother example), "WHO YELLS at Lincoln High" (slide).
- Giraffe: "When you said 'I will tell everyone' yesterday at lunch, I heard…", "When I hear a raised voice in the hallway at Lincoln High, I observe…"
- Rule: If you cannot photograph or record it, it is evaluation, not observation.

### 2. Feeling Without Faux-Feeling

Name the body feeling; reject faux-feelings that hide "you did this to me".

- Real feelings (video): angry, scared, disappointed, hurt, anxious, frustrated, lonely, tense, nervous.
- Faux-feelings (trap): misunderstood, manipulated, judged, rejected, abandoned, betrayed, unheard, ignored — each implies an evaluator.
- Test: After "I feel", can you insert "that I am" or "like I was"? If yes, it is faux. Replace with a real feeling + separate thought.
- Example: "I feel manipulated" → "I feel hurt and anxious because I think I was not consulted."

### 3. Need — Universal, Not Strategy, No "You"

Needs live in everyone and name no person.

- Strategy trap: "I need you to clean the sink" → not a need, it names a person and action. Video correct need: after brother leaves dishes, need is serenity/order when entering the kitchen, not "clean sink". Sink is one strategy among many (others: visual order, shared agreement).
- Need inventory (universal): serenity, order, peace, safety, understanding, respect, trust, privacy, consideration, support, autonomy, connection, care, rest, ease, harmony, partnership.
- Test: Remove "you" and any location/action. If the sentence collapses, it was a strategy. Rewrite to a universal noun.

### 4. Request — Positive, Concrete, Present, Doable, With Choice

Say what you want done, not what you want stopped; add choice.

- Negative fails: "Don't break windows" (38 windows story) — child hears no alternative; window breaks continue because request gives no doable action.
- Positive replaces it: "Would you be willing to play ball in the backyard where windows stay safe?" — concrete, present, doable.
- Vague fails: "I want you to listen to me" (11-year fight) — never defined until reformulated as "Would you be willing to tell me what you heard me say?"
- Criteria: positive (do, not don't), concrete (observable), present (now/next), doable (within power), choice-honoring (accepts no without punishment).
- Phrase: Always start with "Would you be willing to…?" and mean the willingness.
